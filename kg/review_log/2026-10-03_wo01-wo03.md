# Review log: work orders WO01, WO02, WO03 (2026-10-03)

**Reviewer: Claude (an AI), acting for the project owner, who said there is no human reviewer and asked for the batches to be reviewed and
added when confidence is high.** This log is the record of that review. It is deliberately not signed with a person's name.

## How the review was done, and what it is worth

- No evidence row carries a `reviewer` and no claim is `status: reviewed`. The public views treat a reviewer name as "human-reviewed", which
  would be false. Model-found evidence therefore keeps its `llm:` extractor and the scoring layer's x0.6 discount (a single official-document
  source scores 0.54, tier C; two sources 0.79, tier B). Parser-made evidence (`parser:aicrp_rust@1`) has no model in it and keeps full weight.
- Every candidate went through the machine checks first (`agrihub kg ingest-candidates`: source exists on Europe PMC or the URL is readable,
  quote verbatim in the source text, entities resolve, quote names both parties). Then every accepted claim was read against its quote by the
  reviewer, in full (not a sample): 100 % of WO02 and WO03 rows, and for the parser a structural check (ACI never exceeds severity) plus a
  comparison with the model's independent reading.
- Final verification of the 264 new evidence rows against the live documents: 243 exact, 21 identical except for whitespace ("spacing"),
  0 approximate, 0 missing.
- An AI reviewing an AI is a weaker guarantee than a crop scientist reading the claims. Treat tier as the measure of confidence, and a human
  pass (`agrihub kg review-sheet` / `apply-review`) as the way to raise it. Nothing here prevents that later.

## Problems found in my own tooling during this review (all fixed, all tested)

1. `quote_audit.normalise` deleted everything from any `<` to the next `>`: a PDF with "ACI<10" lost whole tables. This made a real table look absent
   and had inflated earlier "not found" counts. (I first wrongly concluded WO01's table was invented; it exists on page 33.)
2. The parser classified by the response letter of the *highest* score. The reports state their own rule ("entries with ACI up to 10.0 were categorized as resistant"),
   and the letter-only reading called `10S (ACI 2.7)` susceptible, creating ~100 false conflicts with the variety notifications. Now: ACI <= 10.0 -> R, otherwise the
   letter; the rule sentence is quoted in every claim; a table without it is not read. The 55 conflicts that remain are real (e.g. MP 4010 stem rust: resistant in its
   2003 notification, ACI 15.8 in the 2022-23 trial).
3. Name matching did not treat en-dash as hyphen, nor "PDKV- Kanak" as "PDKV Kanak"; short table-cell quotes (a name, a year, a state) could not be verified; PDF
   ligature remnants and mid-word splits ("Sriganga nagar") made correct quotes look approximate; the new-variety near-duplicate rule fired on numbered series (NRC 12 / NRC 127).

## Known limits

- Parser claims use the **highest score over hot-spot centres** and ACI averaged over them; the leaf-rust (S)/(N) split is the report's own column grouping. A check variety
  repeated in several AVT groups yields several same-season readings (separate claims, row number in the locator).
- The parser only reads the AICRP Wheat & Barley Crop Protection layout (2021-22 and 2022-23 reports). Later reports were too large for the model's browser and untested here.
- CG 1029 and HI 1634 appear in WO01's tables but are not in the KB (no release metadata), so no claims were made for them.
- Evidence from WO02/WO03 is one source per claim; most claims are tier C by construction.
